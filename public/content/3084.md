r=d−2(d⋅n)n

$$
r = d - 2 \left(\right. d \cdot n \left.\right) n
$$

where d⋅n$d \cdot n$ is the dot product, and n$n$ must be normalized.

Let n^=n∥n∥$\hat{n} = \frac{n}{\parallel n \parallel}$. Then n^$\hat{n}$ is the vector of magnitude one in the same direction as n$n$. The projection of d$d$ in the n$n$ direction is given by p r o j n d=(d⋅n^)n^$\left(p r o j\right)_{n} d = \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n}$, and the projection of d$d$ in the orthogonal direction is therefore given by d−(d⋅n^)n^$d - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n}$. Thus we have

d=(d⋅n^)n^+[d−(d⋅n^)n^]

$$
d = \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} + \left[\right. d - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} \left]\right.
$$

 Note that r$r$ has −1$- 1$ times the projection onto n$n$ that d$d$ has onto n$n$, while the orthogonal projection of r$r$ onto n$n$ is equal to the orthogonal projection of d$d$ onto n$n$, therefore 

r=−(d⋅n^)n^+[d−(d⋅n^)n^]

$$
r = - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} + \left[\right. d - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} \left]\right.
$$

 Alternatively you may look at it as that −r$- r$ has the same projection onto n$n$ that d$d$ has onto n$n$, with its orthogonal projection given by −1$- 1$ times that of d$d$. 

−r=(d⋅n^)n^−[d−(d⋅n^)n^]

$$
- r = \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} - \left[\right. d - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} \left]\right.
$$

 The later equation is exactly 

r=−(d⋅n^)n^+[d−(d⋅n^)n^]

$$
r = - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} + \left[\right. d - \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n} \left]\right.
$$

Hence one can get r$r$ from d$d$ via

r=d−2(d⋅n^)n^

$$
r = d - 2 \left(\right. d \cdot \hat{n} \left.\right) \hat{n}
$$

 Stated in terms of n$n$ itself, this becomes 

r=d−2 d⋅n∥n∥2 n

$$
r = d - \frac{2 d \cdot n}{\parallel n \parallel^{2}} n
$$
