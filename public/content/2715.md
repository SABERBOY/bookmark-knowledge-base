The original 15 members of [Team 5](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Team_5 "Team 5")

*   [Team 5](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Team_5 "Team 5") are the team behind the development of _Hearthstone_. Until near the end of the [closed beta](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Closed_beta "Closed beta"), the team comprised only 15 members, [[1]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Fire-1) the smallest team at Blizzard.[[2]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Initial-2) This allowed them to work much more quickly and in different ways.

*   Team 5 was created specifically for the development of _Hearthstone_, with the intention of taking a different approach to game creation than that previously taken by [Blizzard](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Blizz "wow:Blizz") in developing its games, with 50+ person teams and multi-year development cycles.[[1]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Fire-1) Team 5 was created with the intention of working on a smaller scale but at the same level of quality.[[1]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Fire-1) The mandate for the team was to keep the team very small, and to "think of ways to develop a game that might be non-traditional within Blizzard's walls".[[1]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Fire-1) The smaller team required its members to be "old-school" "garage programmers" and able to "wear a lot of different hats", with far less specialization than that typically found in larger teams.[[1]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Fire-1)

*   _Hearthstone_ development began in 2008, along with the assembling of Team 5.[[3]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-TL-3) However, for a long time Team 5 was a very small group mostly focusing on prototyping.[[4]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-4)[Ben Brode](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Ben_Brode "Ben Brode") states that his first records are from June 2008, but that "full-on development didn't start for a while after that".[[5]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-6) Development by the whole team appears to have started in earnest in spring 2012.[[7]](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone#cite_note-Kotaku-7)_Hearthstone_ was first announced at PAX East in March 2013, reached [beta](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Beta "Beta") in August 2013, and was fully released in March 2014, roughly 6 years after development began.
