## 2026-10-15: JCGT paper v15n2, Pages 49-64

We propose a novel input encoding method of 3D positions for neural networks used in ray tracing algorithms. Our encoding technique utilizes the bounding volume hierarchy (BVH), which serves both as an acceleration data structure for ray tracing and as a spatial data structure for indexing learnable latent feature vectors. Our method naturally adapts to scene geometry, supports general geometric primitives, and maintains a bounded memory footprint independent of scene complexity. We demonstrate an application of our technique in the context of neural radiance cache (NRC).

## 2026-08-05: JCGT paper v15n2, Pages 12-48

Two-dimensional mappings between discs and squares find applications in computer graphics, mathematical art, cartography, and engineering. This article introduces and benchmarks novel parametric mappings that send concentric circular contours on the disc onto Fernández-Guasti squircles of suitably chosen squareness and scale on the square. The presented mappings abandon the radial constraint of Fong squircle mapping and instead impose highly specific polar angle dependencies that make them either rigorously area-preserving or quasi angle-preserving.

The equal-area variants are governed by transcendental equations with incomplete elliptic functions. They are 11–17x slower than Shirley mapping but offer notably better shape preservation along the diagonals and 1000x lower angle distortion in the central region, at still reasonable performance on a modern laptop (under 2 seconds for a 1000 x 1000 image).

The quasi-conformal variants involve only elementary functions with real arguments. An optimal incarnation achieving average angle distortion below 0.8 degrees is nearly indistinguishable from truly conformal Schwarz–Christoffel transformation while running up to 12% faster.

Squircle-to-squircle mapping (from any squareness to any squareness) can be achieved by concatenating a square-to-disc and disc-to-square mapping with suitably chosen pre- and post-scalings. This opens up novel animation opportunities: for example, a square image can be smoothly and continuously morphed into a circular one or vice versa through a sequence of intermediate squircular stages that preserves either relative areas (rigorously) or local angles (with very good approximation) across the entire shape at each frame of the transition.

Finally, concatenating a forward and reverse squircle-based mapping with purposefully mismatched parameter values enables easily tuneable warping effects on the disc and square.

C++ and Octave source code with efficient computer implementations of all discussed mappings and associated warping effects are provided as supplementary material.

## 2026-07-28: JCGT paper v15n2, Pages 1-11

We present a texture-based technique for evaluating Bézier curves on the GPU that leverages fixed-function linear texture interpolation hardware. By offloading curve evaluation to the texture interpolator, this approach can improve performance in compute-bound GPU workloads. The method can also be used naturally for Bézier surfaces and volumes and extends to advanced curve types such as B-splines, NURBS, and both integral and rational polynomials. We show how Seiler interpolation fits into this framework to improve efficiency. We also compare performance and accuracy against curves evaluated as polynomials in shader code.

## 2026-05-22: JCGT paper v15n1, Pages 60-75

[![Image 2](https://jcgt.org/published/0015/01/04/icon.png)](https://jcgt.org/published/0015/01/04/)Jamie Portsmouth _Autodesk_ Matthias Raab _NVIDIA_ Laurent Belcour _Intel_ Francis Liu _NVIDIA_

We present the Minimal Retroreflective Microfacet (MRM) model, which turns any existing microfacet BSDF into a physically plausible retroreflective one by a single substitution: replacing the view direction with its reflection about the surface normal before evaluating the standard model. Based on the previously published back-vector formulation, MRM requires only minimal code changes and has been adopted in the OpenPBR and MaterialX material standards. We prove reciprocity and energy conservation under the assumption of a reflection-symmetric normal distribution function (NDF), which holds for all commonly used distributions, and validate the model against measured retroreflective material data.
